# AI Explorers — Curriculum Preview

This document gives a readable overview of the learning material currently in the app. Each topic has two versions: one for ages 5–9 and one for ages 10–15.

Every lesson currently includes:
- A short explanation
- A real-life example
- A question to think about
- A multiple-choice challenge with feedback

## Learning path: Ages 5–9

| # | Lesson | What the learner explores |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI is all around us | Recognizing AI in maps, music apps and voice helpers. Understanding that AI is technology made by people, not magic. |
| 2 | How machines learn | Learning that computers can find patterns from examples, and that they need examples of different things. |
| 3 | Training data detectives | Understanding training data and why examples should be varied and checked. |
| 4 | Finding patterns | Seeing how AI can group or compare information without understanding it like a person. |
| 5 | Inside a neural network | Exploring connected mathematical parts and how information passes through layers. |
| 6 | How AI works with words | Meeting large language models and learning that fluent answers can still be wrong. |
| 7 | Prompts and context | Giving clear instructions and useful details to help an AI understand a task. |
| 8 | AI with extra information | Understanding how an AI can look up useful notes or documents before answering (RAG). |
| 9 | AI assistants and agents | Comparing an assistant that replies with an agent that can use tools, and why permission matters. |
| 10 | Reasoning, mistakes and safety | Checking surprising answers and asking a trusted adult or source when needed. |

### Sample lesson — How machines learn

**Explanation:** Machine learning is one way computers learn from examples. People choose the examples and the learning method, then test what the computer learned.

**Example:** Show a computer many pictures of cats and dogs. It can learn patterns that help sort new pictures.

**Think about it:** If it sees only cats, will it know what a dog looks like?

**Challenge:** What helps a machine-learning system learn?
- Examples to learn from (correct)
- A magic button
- Only one guess

## Learning path: Ages 10–15

| # | Lesson | What the learner explores |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI is all around us | Distinguishing AI from ordinary software and understanding that real products often combine models, rules and data feeds. |
| 2 | How machines learn | Understanding model training, evaluation and generalization to examples not seen during training. |
| 3 | Training data detectives | Exploring data quality, coverage, representation, consent and the risk of uneven performance. |
| 4 | Finding patterns | Comparing supervised learning, unsupervised learning and reinforcement learning. |
| 5 | Inside a neural network | Understanding layers, weights, parameters, training objectives and deep learning. |
| 6 | How AI works with words | Learning how large language models predict tokens and why fluent output is not proof of accuracy. |
| 7 | Prompts and context | Writing clearer instructions with audience, constraints and useful context. |
| 8 | AI with extra information | Understanding retrieval-augmented generation, retrieval quality and grounded answers. |
| 9 | AI assistants and agents | Exploring models, tools, state, action loops, permissions and human approval. |
| 10 | Reasoning, mistakes and safety | Evaluating model outputs, verifying assumptions and applying privacy and human oversight. |

### Sample lesson — How machines learn

**Explanation:** Machine learning is a branch of AI in which a model improves at a task by learning patterns from data rather than relying only on hand-written rules. Training and evaluation are separate steps.

**Example:** A model learns to classify spam using labelled email examples, then is tested on emails it has not seen.

**Think about it:** Why should we test with examples the model did not train on?

**Challenge:** Why keep test examples separate from training data?
- To measure generalization (correct)
- To make training slower
- To hide the labels

## Topics and concepts covered

- AI recognition and everyday uses
- Machine learning
- Training data and data quality
- Supervised and unsupervised learning
- Neural networks and deep learning
- Large language models (LLMs) and tokens
- Prompts and context
- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- AI agents and tool use
- Limitations, mistakes, fairness, privacy and human oversight

## Current scope and next work

The app currently has the two age paths, ten lesson topics per path, ten quiz challenges per path, feedback, and a browser-local progress indicator.

The next content and product work should include:
- Hands-on experiments where learners can change inputs and observe outcomes
- More visual explanations and step-by-step diagrams
- Additional practice questions and mixed review challenges
- Accessibility and usability review with the intended age groups
- A proper build and browser test before publishing a live preview
